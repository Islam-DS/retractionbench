# Notice length vs. notice rank (ADR-081, updated ADR-088/090/092)

**Status: exploratory, not pre-registered.** This test was prompted by ADR-077's observation
that RB-0007 and RB-0008 -- both with a confirmed-`bare`, fully empty notice -- rank their
notice *above* their trap source, the opposite of every other item. It is a post-hoc hypothesis
tested against the same items that motivated it, not an independent confirmation. Read the
correlation below as suggestive, not as a finding, at any n reported.

## Method

For every benchmark item with a separately retrievable notice document (12 of 13 -- RB-0005 has
none, ADR-071, and is excluded rather than assigned a rank), the indexed notice text is
reconstructed exactly as `corpus/embed_index.py` embeds it:

```
f"Notice: {source.title}" + " " + (source.notice_text or "")
```

character length is measured, and correlated against `notice_rank` from
`results/retrieval_ceiling_report.json`.

## Result

| n | Pearson r | Spearman rho |
|---|---|---|
| 8 (original, ADR-081) | 0.7985 | 0.7715 |
| 11 (ADR-088) | 0.7522 | 0.7396 |
| 12 (current, ADR-092) | 0.7142 | 0.6828 |

The correlation has weakened gradually as more points were added (0.80 -> 0.75 -> 0.71) but has
not collapsed -- still a moderate-to-strong positive relationship, consistent with regression
toward a more representative sample rather than the original n=8 result being a fluke. RB-0013
(item 21, Colorado elevation/HDP) is also `bare` (134 chars) but ranks 2, worse than its trap
source -- like RB-0002, it is a counterexample to any reading of "bare" as a sufficient
condition for ranking above the trap source. Of 5 `bare`-notice items now recorded, 3
(RB-0007, RB-0008, RB-0011) rank above their trap source and 2 (RB-0002, RB-0013) do not --
consistent with the continuous, not threshold, reading below.

## Interpretation -- mechanism investigated directly (ADR-090), not asserted as a finding

**The mechanism is a generic lexical-overlap property of dense retrieval, not a finding about**
**supersession or about notice informativeness.** Investigated directly against the actual
embedded documents (ADR-090, `results/notice_length_mechanism.md`): the three
notice-above-trap-source items have a notice that is *exactly* the retracted paper's title and
nothing else, sharing near-total vocabulary with a question written about that same paper.
Three contrasting below-cases add several hundred characters of retraction-notice boilerplate
that shares no vocabulary with the question. A bare notice ranks well because it is nearly
identical, word for word, to the question; an informative notice ranks worse because additional
text uncorrelated with the question's vocabulary dilutes cosine similarity, regardless of what
that text says. **This is not evidence that informative notices are penalized for being**
**informative** -- the same effect would appear for any short-vs-long document pair on any
topic. What remains a real, separate, practical finding is that most notices in this pool *are*
short (ADR-084: 66.4% under 200 characters), which is the premise the correlation needs to
matter broadly, independent of why the mechanism produces the pattern it does.

**This is not asserted as a finding here.** n=12 is still far too small, the test was not
pre-registered, and it was generated by inspecting the same two (now five) items it is being
checked against. See ADR-081 for the decision to record it as a tracked hypothesis and
formally pre-register a proper test at full benchmark size, rather than re-testing it
informally each time new items are added -- ADR-088 and ADR-092 each continue that informal
re-test one more time; a formal pre-registered version, ideally with an explicit mechanism
test (lexical overlap between question and notice, measured directly, not proxied by length
alone), is still owed.
